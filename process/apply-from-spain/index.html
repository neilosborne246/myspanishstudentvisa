What Is Cambio de Estatus?

What Is Cambio de Estatus — Spain Student Visa From Inside Spain

Cambio de estatus (change of status) — also referred to as modificación de estancia — is the legal process of changing your immigration status within Spain without leaving the country. Instead of applying at a Spanish consulate abroad, you apply directly at the Oficina de Extranjería (foreigners' office) of the Spanish province where you will study.

This route is governed by article 54 of Spain's immigration regulation (Real Decreto 1155/2024). Applying from inside Spain is open to two groups: anyone who already holds a Spanish residence authorisation, and adults lawfully in Spain enrolling in higher education (university degrees, official and university master's and PhD programmes and the Máster de Formación Profesional) or specialised health training. You must not have overstayed, and you must meet all the same document requirements as a standard student visa applicant. Everyone else, including language-course students, applies through a Spanish consulate abroad. It is a legitimate pathway but one that requires careful timing and expert handling.

⚠ Important Warning — Read This First

The cambio de estatus route is more complex than applying from your home country. Filing extends your lawful situation while the application is decided, but if it is refused — or no decision is notified within two months, which counts as a refusal — you must leave Spain promptly; overstaying can lead to a fine or expulsion and affect future travel to the EU. This route should only be pursued if you are confident in meeting all conditions and ideally with specialist immigration guidance. If you have not yet travelled to Spain, apply from your home country instead.

Eligibility Conditions

Conditions You Must Meet to Apply from Inside Spain

All five conditions must be met simultaneously. Missing any one of them makes the cambio de estatus route unavailable to you.

1

You Are Currently in Spain Legally

You must have entered Spain through a legitimate Schengen crossing with a valid entry stamp or right of entry. You cannot be in Spain irregularly or with an expired entry.

2

You Have Not Overstayed Your 90-Day Schengen Allowance

Non-EU nationals are permitted a maximum of 90 days in the Schengen Area within any 180-day period. Your cambio de estatus application must be submitted at least two months before this allowance expires.

3

You Are an Adult Enrolled in Higher Education or Specialised Health Training

Unless you already hold a Spanish residence authorisation, you must be 18 or over and enrolled in a university degree, official or university master's or PhD programme, the Máster de Formación Profesional, or specialised health training. Language courses do not qualify. The application must be filed at least two months before the course starts.

4

You Have All Required Documents

The same document set as a standard student visa application is required: enrolment letter, financial proof, health insurance, criminal record with apostille and sworn translation, medical certificate, and accommodation proof.

5

You Submit Two Months Before Your Stay Ends

The application must be submitted at the Oficina de Extranjería of the province where you will study at least two months before your current lawful stay ends — on a 90-day visa-free stay, that means within roughly the first month. Filing in the final weeks of your stay is too late.

Comparison

Applying From Home vs Applying From Spain — Which Route Is Right for You?

Most applicants are better served by applying from their home country. Here is an honest comparison to help you decide.

✓ Applying from Home Country

  • Simpler and more predictable process
  • No risk of Schengen overstay during processing
  • Full time to prepare all documents without pressure
  • You can travel and use your passport until appointment
  • Consulate staff experienced with standard applications
  • Processing outcome does not affect your legal presence in Spain
  • Recommended for the vast majority of applicants

⚠ Applying from Inside Spain (Cambio de Estatus)

  • More complex — higher risk of procedural errors
  • You cannot leave Spain while the application is pending
  • Schengen day pressure — must submit at least two months before your stay ends
  • A refusal (or no answer within two months) puts your legal status at risk
  • Filed with the Oficina de Extranjería of the province where you will study
  • Decided within two months — no answer counts as a refusal
  • Only appropriate in specific circumstances
